# Togo Independent Bookshop Network

## Data Operations Handbook for Reading Clubs and Retail Branches

The Togo Independent Bookshop Network coordinates book sales, reading-club memberships, author events, and stock transfers among participating shops in Lome, Kara, and Sokode. The network's data ecosystem includes branch staff, publishers, reading-club organizers, customers, and the central catalogue team.

Roles are known and identified for all actors. Cashiers are the data producers for sales records, club organizers produce attendance lists, publishers supply title metadata, and branch managers are data users who review weekly summaries. The central catalogue manager is the coordinator and is responsible for resolving duplicate book records and maintaining the shared classification list.

The network seeks diversity and representativeness among its data producers and users by including large shops, small neighborhood sellers, mobile booksellers, student reading groups, adult reading circles, and publishers working in French and local languages. Each participating branch names one staff member as its data steward.

Human capacity for data management is maintained through quarterly training on data entry, spreadsheet review, catalogue documentation, and dashboard interpretation. New staff complete a practical exercise before receiving access. Written procedures define responsibilities for collection, quality assurance, correction, approval, and publication of the monthly retail summary.

## Data Supply, Quality, and Production

The shared catalogue makes book-title, author, format, price, branch-stock, order, sale, and reading-club attendance data available to participating shops. Authorized users can access current records through a browser and can obtain a weekly export when they need to work offline.

Data quality checks cover accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, and validity. ISBN values are checked against the publisher file; required title and price fields must be complete; branch codes use one consistent format; sales are uploaded by noon on the following business day; and invalid negative quantities are rejected. Monthly reports disaggregate sales by branch, language, book format, and customer programme.

Branch supervisors compare a sample of receipts with uploaded records and document every correction. A completeness dashboard flags missing daily files, while a consistency rule identifies the same title recorded under different spellings. Records that fail validation remain in a review queue until the producing branch corrects them.

The technical infrastructure consists of a central catalogue platform, a sales repository, encrypted branch connections, an access-control service, and scheduled backups. Common metadata standards define title identifiers, contributor names, language codes, formats, and branch identifiers. An application programming interface supports technical and data interoperability between point-of-sale tools and the catalogue.

## Data Demand, Applications, and Use Cases

Managers use sales and stock data to decide which novels, schoolbooks, comics, and poetry collections to reorder. Reading-club organizers use registration and attendance data to choose meeting times, room sizes, and discussion themes. Publishers use aggregated order data to plan print runs, and mobile booksellers use branch-level demand summaries to select titles for weekend stalls.

The network reviews whether data supply meets demand during a monthly user meeting. Users can request a new field or report, and the catalogue team records the request, identifies the decision it supports, tests the proposed change with two branches, and reports whether it was adopted. The current dashboard was revised after users asked for separate views of reservations, completed sales, returns, and unsold stock.

An AI-enabled recommendation workflow suggests books from a customer's selected genres and prior purchases. The technical team evaluates recommendation accuracy, documents the model version, and requires a staff member to approve every display list used at a public event. The workflow is limited to retail recommendations and reading-club selections.

Weekly analyses compare available stock with customer orders so that transfers can be arranged between branches. A branch with excess copies can supply a branch where demand is higher. The resulting transfer report records the title, quantity, sending branch, receiving branch, request date, and completion date.

## Governance, Resources, and Collaboration

Data governance policies describe how retail and membership data is collected, managed, shared, protected, and used across network actors. The steering group approves common rules, reviews compliance each quarter, and assigns accountability for corrections and access decisions. An audit log records changes to prices, stock quantities, and user permissions.

Privacy rules prohibit branches from sharing named customer histories with publishers or other customers. Membership contact details are visible only to authorized organizers, and customers may request correction or deletion. Security measures include individual accounts, multifactor authentication, encryption, monthly access reviews, and a documented process for reporting a lost shop laptop.

The three-year data strategy sets a vision for a reliable shared catalogue and consistent retail reporting. A dedicated budget covers hosting, staff training, backup storage, and maintenance of branch connections. Participating shops receive a reduced annual network fee when they contribute complete files on time, providing an incentive mechanism for ecosystem contributions.

Community engagement takes place through reading circles, customer panels, and public book-selection meetings. The network communicates planned catalogue changes in French, Ewe, and Kabiyè and invites local organizers to test new membership forms. Collaboration agreements and partnerships with publishers, libraries, schools, and bookseller associations specify which aggregated retail data may be exchanged and which organization is responsible for each joint activity.
