# Togo Retail Logistics Platform: Infrastructure and Partnership Review

The Togo Retail Logistics Platform is a private commercial service used by participating wholesalers, shops, transport companies, and warehouse operators in Lome and Kara. It records product orders, invoices, warehouse stock, delivery confirmations, and customer returns. The service is intended exclusively for routine retail logistics and commercial administration.

The participating companies have defined complementary roles. Shops submit orders, wholesalers confirm product availability, transport companies update delivery status, and warehouse operators record incoming and outgoing stock. A private platform operator coordinates the technical service and maintains the shared product catalogue. Monthly collaboration meetings bring these organisations together with the software vendor and telecommunications provider to discuss service performance.

The partners use a common product identifier and agreed metadata fields for item name, supplier, package size, price, warehouse location, and delivery status. Application programming interfaces support interoperability between shop-ordering software, warehouse scanners, and the central platform. Managers use dashboards to compare stock levels with customer orders and to decide when commercial goods should be transferred between warehouses.

# Data Security, Vulnerabilities, and Risk Controls

A technical review identified cybersecurity vulnerabilities in two warehouse terminals, including outdated software and shared user accounts. The operator classified these findings as risks to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of commercial records. Remedial work introduced individual accounts, multifactor authentication, encrypted connections, security logging, monthly access reviews, and automated backups.

The security policy assigns accountability for approving access, investigating unusual activity, documenting incidents, and restoring data. Customer contact details are subject to privacy controls, purpose limitation, role-based access, and scheduled deletion. A penetration test is commissioned annually, while identified vulnerabilities are entered in a risk register with an owner, deadline, and verification status.

The infrastructure consists of a cloud-hosted database, warehouse scanners, encrypted network connections, an access-control service, and offline export files used when a shop has an unreliable connection. A tested recovery procedure can restore the commercial database after equipment failure. These controls address cyber risk, service interruption, fraud, and accidental loss of retail records.

# Governance, Capacity, Data Quality, and Use

A steering committee representing the participating businesses approves data-governance rules and reviews compliance. Written procedures describe data collection, validation, correction, sharing, retention, and deletion. Data-quality checks examine completeness, accuracy, consistency, timeliness, and validity. Missing order numbers are rejected, product codes must use the common format, and warehouse supervisors compare a sample of delivery records with signed receipts.

Staff receive capacity-building sessions on data entry, dashboard interpretation, privacy, cyber hygiene, and incident reporting. The platform budget includes dedicated resources for hosting, security testing, training, software maintenance, and replacement scanners. Small retailers pay a reduced participation fee during their first year, and user representatives can request changes to fields and reports.

The software vendor is piloting an AI tool that predicts which products may need restocking. Human managers must review every recommendation before placing an order. The pilot records the model version, prediction accuracy, and user feedback, and the steering committee may discontinue it if the recommendations do not support commercial purchasing decisions.

The partnership review concluded that continued communication, clearly assigned responsibilities, common standards, staff capacity, privacy safeguards, security controls, and reliable infrastructure were necessary for the platform's commercial purpose.
